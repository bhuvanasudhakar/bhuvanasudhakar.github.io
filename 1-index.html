On cafes

In Chennai, coffee is never just coffee

Nobody in Chennai grabs a coffee and runs. Even in the busiest new cafes, people sit. They talk. They order one cold coffee and stretch it over three hours of conversation, and nobody minds.

Every neighbourhood has its own favourite. The old place near the beach where the filter coffee is strong enough to wake the dead. The tiny new one with fairy lights and a chalk menu, where the owner remembers your order by your second visit.

What I love most is how the old city and the new one share the same table. You can sit with your laptop and an oat milk latte, and right outside the window an anna sells sundal from his cart, same as he has for twenty years. Nobody finds this strange. That is Chennai for you.

So if you visit, skip the five-star coffee. Find the small cafe with the wobbly table and the good playlist. Order whatever the next table is having. You will not regret it.

On weddings

Planning a South Indian wedding (and staying sane)

Nobody warns you that planning a South Indian wedding is a full-time job. I found out the hard way.

It starts innocently. One muhurtham date from the family astrologer, and suddenly there are four hundred guests, a hall to book, a caterer who answers everything with 'don't worry, madam', and a saree list longer than my arm. Mine is in December, in a small town in Tamil Nadu, and every auntie in the family has an opinion on every detail. The flowers. The sweets. Whether the nadaswaram should play before or after the couple enters. (Before. It is before.)

But here is what nobody tells you either. In the middle of all the chaos, there are moments that make it worth it. Watching your mother drape her own wedding saree 'just to check'. The smell of malligai poo in the hall at five in the morning. Your grandmother correcting the cook on how much jaggery goes into the payasam.

If you are planning your own wedding right now, my only advice is this. Decide the two or three things you truly care about, and let the rest go. Years later, people remember the food and how happy the couple looked. Nobody goes home talking about the stage decoration.

On Instagram for small businesses

Five things running a business page taught me

I run the Instagram page for my own small business, and I handle outreach for a tech startup on the side. I am not a guru. But here is what has actually worked for me.

1. Answer DMs fast, and answer like a person. Not 'Thank you for your enquiry, we will revert shortly.' Just 'Hi! Yes, we can do that. Tell me a little about what you need.' A friendly reply in ten minutes wins more work than a perfect reply tomorrow.

2. Show the real work, not the polished ad. My best posts are the simple behind-the-scenes ones. A work in progress. A before and after. People trust what looks real.

3. The first three seconds of a reel decide its fate. If nothing interesting happens before the viewer's thumb moves, the reel is gone. Open with the best moment, not your logo.

4. Show up every week. Three good posts a week, every week, will grow a page faster than fifteen posts in one week and a month of silence after. Followers learn when to expect you. So does the app.

5. Talk like a human in your captions. Say it out loud before you post. If it sounds like something out of a company brochure, bin it and write what you would actually say to a customer standing in front of you.

None of this needs money. It needs patience and a slightly thick skin. That is honestly the whole secret.
